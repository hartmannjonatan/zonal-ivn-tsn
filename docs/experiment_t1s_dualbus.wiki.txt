!! __Context__
* __Field.__ In-vehicle networks (IVN) are moving from domain architectures with many CAN/LIN/FlexRay buses to zonal architectures: a central compute (HPC) and a few zonal switches joined by a TSN Ethernet backbone. Each zonal switch collects the sensors and actuators of its physical zone.
* __Why it is relevant.__ Zonal architectures cut wiring weight and cost only if the last metre to cheap sensors and actuators is also cheap. 10BASE-T1S (IEEE 802.3cg, 10 Mb/s over one twisted pair, multidrop) with PLCA (Physical Layer Collision Avoidance) is the Ethernet candidate to replace CAN at the edge, so the whole car speaks Ethernet end to end with no gateways.
* __Open problems in the field.__
** PLCA gives each node one transmit opportunity (TO) per round-robin cycle. Latency depends on the cycle length and on the node position in it, not on the IEEE 802.1Q priority (PCP) of the frame.
** TSN tools (802.1Qbv TAS, 802.1AS gPTP) are defined for point-to-point Ethernet. How they combine with a shared PLCA segment is not standardized; INET's gPTP, for example, cannot run over a multidrop link.
** Control loops that cross zones (a sensor on one T1S bus read by an ECU on another bus) cross two PLCA segments plus the switched backbone. The latency and jitter of that path have not been quantified for realistic automotive traffic.
* __Problem addressed here.__ Can safety-relevant control traffic (PCP 7, the highest class of the P1 catalog) cross from one 10BASE-T1S bus to another through the zonal TSN network within the 1 ms reference that IEEE 802.1DG contributions give for safety-relevant control, and how much of the jitter can scheduling and time synchronization remove?
* __How others addressed it so far.__ The baseline is Jonatan's zonal IVN TSN model (OMNeT++ 6.4 / INET 4.7, P1 traffic catalog, campaigns C0-C4 with CBS/TAS and gPTP), where every endpoint has a point-to-point 100 Mb/s or 1 Gb/s link. INET ships a PLCA model (-+EthernetPlca+-, -+EthernetPlcaInterface+-) but no zonal or automotive use of it. Jean's -+plca-priority-to+- work adds priority TOs to PLCA.
* __What is still missing.__ A study of T1S segments inside a complete zonal TSN model, with cross-bus request/response traffic at the top priority and real (drifting, gPTP-corrected) clocks.

!! __Problem Statement__
In a zonal TSN in-vehicle network, a highest-priority (PCP 7) control exchange between ECUs and sensors on two different 10BASE-T1S PLCA buses crosses two PLCA segments and the switched backbone. PLCA ignores frame priority, so the latency and jitter of this path are set by the PLCA cycle phase and are unknown, and it is not known whether TSN scheduling and gPTP can bound them to the 1 ms reference for safety-relevant control.

!! __Envisioned Solution__
* __Formal proof?__ No. A single-segment analytic PLCA bound exists (README_t1s F2); the cross-bus bound is future work.
* __Implementation and experimental validation?__ No hardware. The solution is validated by simulation.
* __Simulation model.__ Extend Jonatan's OMNeT++/INET zonal model: replace the point-to-point WINDOW links in zones Z1 and Z3 with two 10BASE-T1S PLCA segments and attach realistic powertrain ECUs and sensors to them. All other links, flows (P1 catalog) and switch settings stay as in the original.
* __What is simulated.__ Cross-bus request/response and one-way flows at PCP 7 next to the original P1 traffic, under (1) random release phases, (2) synchronized releases plus a TAS gate that holds the low-priority window traffic, (3) the same schedule with drifting clocks, with and without gPTP. Topology variant: both buses on one zonal switch. Second scenario: electric vehicle.
* __Statistical significance.__ 10 seeds (1001-1010, as in the official P1 campaign) x 30 s of measurement per configuration, 30 000 to 300 000 samples per flow. Per flow: mean, p99, max, delay variation (max - min), standard deviation, inter-arrival jitter, samples above 1 ms, losses.

!! __Hypothesis__
In a zonal TSN network with two 10BASE-T1S PLCA buses, PCP 7 control traffic between the buses can be bounded below the 1 ms safety-relevant control reference, even under stress load, when releases are synchronized and the T1S coordinator port gates the lower-priority traffic for part of each 1 ms cycle. gPTP-synchronized zone clocks are enough to hold that schedule, and the jitter that remains comes from the PLCA cycle phase, not from clock quality.

!! __Demonstration Strategy__ (aka Methodology)
* __How the hypothesis is demonstrated.__ Simulation in Jonatan's model, changing only what PLCA needs and leaving the P1 catalog, the backbone and the switch configuration as they are. Every new network and configuration is generated by a script into new files; the original configurations are never edited.
* __Assets already available.__
** Literature: IEEE 802.3cg (clause 148, PLCA), 802.1Q (TAS), 802.1AS (gPTP), IEEE P802.1DG contributions (1 ms for safety-relevant control), SAE J1939 message periods.
** Tools: OMNeT++ 6.4.0, INET 4.7 (-+EthernetPlca+-, -+EthernetPlcaInterface+-, -+EthernetMultidropLink+-, -+Ieee8021qTimeAwareShaper+-, gPTP, -+OscillatorBasedClock+-), Python for generation and analysis.
** Previous works by the group: Jonatan's -+zonal-ivn-tsn+- repository (network -+ZonalIVN+-, P1 catalog, C0-C4 campaigns, C4 gPTP hierarchy and clock model); Jean's -+PrioPlca+- (-+plca-priority-to+-).
** Skills: OMNeT++/INET modelling, TSN configuration, Python data analysis.

!!! How PLCA was added to Jonatan's model
# __Validation step (one T1S segment per zone, C0 comparison).__ -+ZonalIVNT1S+- is a copy of -+ZonalIVN+- in which the 100 Mb/s WINDOW and RADAR links of each zone become one T1S segment. Catalog C0 on both networks gave identical results for every flow not on T1S and +48 to +52 µs for the radar (10 Mb/s serialization plus waiting for the TO), so the PLCA integration does not disturb the rest of the model (-+README_t1s.md+-, steps 1-5).
# __Two T1S buses.__ The generator -+gen_t1s_powertrain.py+- copies -+ZonalIVN.ned+- into -+ZonalIVNT1SPowertrain.ned+- and changes only zones Z1 and Z3:
## The zonal switch port that served the WINDOW node (Z1 -+eth[5]+-, Z3 -+eth[6]+-) becomes an -+EthernetPlcaInterface+- with PLCA node ID 0: the zonal switch is the PLCA __coordinator__ and sends the BEACON.
## That port, the WINDOW node and four new nodes connect through -+EthernetMultidropLink+- (0.5-1 m) to a -+WireJunction+-, which models the shared twisted pair.
## Bus A (Z1, engine): coordinator 0, WINDOW1 1, ECM 2, ETC (throttle) 3, MAP 4, LAMBDA 5. Bus B (Z3, transmission): coordinator 0, WINDOW3 1, TCU 2, APP (pedal) 3, GEAR 4, OSS (output speed) 5. Each new node is a -+TsnDevice+- with one -+EthernetPlcaInterface+-.
## PLCA parameters: 10 Mb/s, -+plca_node_count = 6+-, -+to_timer_length = 32b+-, -+max_bc = 0+- (no burst). The node count equals the number of nodes, so the cycle has no empty TO.
## The queue of the coordinator port and of the ECUs is an -+Ieee8021qTimeAwareShaper+-: 8 traffic classes, PCP n to TC n, strict priority, gates open unless a schedule is set. Inside a node PCP 7 frames always go first; on the wire every node still gets only one TO per cycle.
## Static forwarding entries (VLAN 40) are added in the central switch and in Z1/Z3 so that frames to the new nodes reach the T1S port.
# __Traffic.__ All powertrain frames are 64 B (8 B payload), VLAN 40, PCP 7, the class of the brake flows in P1. The original catalog keeps running on the same network. Request/response flows use an INET -+UdpApp+- client with an -+RttSink+- and a -+UdpEchoApp+- server, so the round-trip time (RTT) crosses both buses twice.

# __Variants.__
## Random phases (baseline): each source starts at a uniform random offset within its period.
## Coordinator relief: PLCA burst (-+max_bc = 3+-) on the coordinator, and -+PrioPlca+- in -+multiTo+- mode (coordinator gets every other TO).
## Synchronized + TAS 400 µs: fixed release offsets in every 1 ms (cross-bus flows at 10-80 µs, local loops at 450-660 µs) and the window traffic class (TC3) gate on both coordinator ports closed for the first 400 µs of every 1 ms. Gates of 700 and 800 µs were tried and lose window frames.
## Real clocks: every clock is an -+OscillatorBasedClock+- with constant drift from U(-50, +50) ppm, as in C4. With gPTP, the C4 hierarchy keeps the zonal switches aligned (HPC grandmaster, central switch bridge, zonal switches slaves, Sync 125 ms, Pdelay 1 s). Without gPTP the zones run free. T1S nodes use the clock of their zonal switch, because INET's gPTP needs point-to-point links and cannot run over the multidrop segment.
## gPTP sensitivity: Sync 31.25 ms; drift ±10 and ±100 ppm.
## Same switch: both buses on Z1 (8 access ports instead of 7; bus B on a new port -+eth[9]+- with 5 nodes).
## Second scenario: electric vehicle (bus A: VCU, front inverter, DC/DC, thermal; bus B: BMS, rear inverter, OBC, ultrasonic), same method.
# __Measurement.__ 1 s warm-up, 30 s measurement, seeds 1001-1010 per configuration. Latency from INET -+endToEndDelay+- (one-way) and -+roundTripTime+- (RTT) vectors; window losses from the window sink counters.
* __What is still missing.__
** gPTP inside the T1S segment (only between zones today).
** PLCA cycle aligned to the gPTP time base, which should remove the cycle-phase jitter that is left.
** An analytic worst-case bound for the cross-bus path.
** A fix for the -+PrioPlca multiTo+- crash seen on some dual-bus runs.
* __How to tackle the missing elements.__ Align the coordinator BEACON to gPTP time in a -+PrioPlca+- variant; extend the single-segment bound of README_t1s F2 to two segments plus the backbone; debug -+multiTo+- with a debug build and event log.
* __Resources needed.__ One 8 GB notebook (WSL); a 30 s run takes about 2 h with 18 in parallel. A larger machine would shorten the campaigns.
* __Who can help and how.__ Advisor: review of the method and choice of results for the Bosch workshop. Jonatan: check that the T1S integration respects the P1 model assumptions.
* __How long it will take.__ About 6 weeks for the open items (see schedule).

!!! Results so far
The hypothesis holds in the powertrain scenario. With random phases the cross-bus RTT under stress reaches 1.21 ms (2018 samples above 1 ms). With synchronized releases, a 400 µs TAS gate and gPTP, every cross-bus flow stays at or below 0.85 ms, with no losses. Without gPTP the same schedule is worse than random and the window traffic loses 219 427 frames.

* __Latency without scheduling.__ One-way cross-bus 170-680 µs and RTT 450-1210 µs; no period is ever missed (fig6, fig7).
* __Jitter between the buses.__ With random phases the delay variation (max - min) is 190-930 µs, set by where the frame lands in the PLCA cycle of each bus (fig8).
* __Coordinator bottleneck.__ The zonal switch is one node of the cycle and gets one TO per cycle for all the frames it forwards into the bus. PLCA burst (-+max_bc = 3+-) halves window losses at 8 nodes but raises cross-bus latency; -+PrioPlca multiTo+- gives mixed results and crashes on some runs (fig9).
* __Scheduling.__ Synchronized releases + TAS 400 µs cut the delay variation 2-5x and bring every flow under 0.61 ms with ideal clocks (fig10, fig11).
* __Real clocks.__ gPTP holds the schedule; the standard deviation stays between the random and ideal cases. Sync 31.25 ms or drift ±10/±100 ppm change it by less than 3 µs, so the remaining jitter is PLCA cycle phase, not clock error (fig12, fig13).
* __Electric vehicle.__ The torque command EV01 drops from 571 to 242 µs max with gPTP scheduling, but flows released late in the 1 ms cycle get worse (EV03 RTT 966 µs), so offsets must be chosen per flow (fig13).
* __Same switch.__ Both buses on Z1 remove one switch hop: max latency drops by 24-326 µs and the stress case stays below 1 ms even without scheduling; the standard deviation does not change (fig14).

!! __Bibliography__
* __IEEE 802.3cg-2019__ (10BASE-T1S, clause 148 PLCA). Defines the multidrop PHY, the BEACON, transmit opportunities, to_timer and burst mode reproduced through INET's EthernetPlca. It explains why PLCA latency depends on the cycle and not on frame priority, the root of the problem statement.
* __IEEE 802.1Q__ (time-aware shaper, 802.1Qbv). Basis of the TC3 gate on the coordinator port that frees the PLCA cycle for PCP 7 traffic.
* __IEEE 802.1AS__ (gPTP). Synchronizes the zonal switches in the real-clock variants; its restriction to point-to-point links is why time inside each T1S segment is taken from the zonal switch.
* __IEEE P802.1DG__ (TSN profile for automotive in-vehicle Ethernet) and its working-group contributions. Source of the 1 ms reference for safety-relevant control used as the latency target.
* __SAE J1939__ (TSC1, EEC1, ETC1). Gives the realistic 10 ms periods of the cross-bus powertrain messages.
* __Jonatan, zonal-ivn-tsn and P1-final specification.__ The zonal IVN model, P1 traffic catalog and C0-C4 campaigns that this work extends; results are comparable with its official seeds and settings.
* __J. C. Macedo, plca-priority-to (PrioPlca).__ Priority and multi-TO PLCA variants used in the coordinator-relief study.
* __OMNeT++ 6.4 and INET 4.7.__ Simulation framework and models.
